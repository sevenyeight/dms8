A landscape is a feeling first.  
I'm not painting the scene —   
I'm painting what it did to me   
when I stood in front of it. 

There's a longing in it too.   
Something about solitude.   
The quiet of a place that doesn't need you in it.


And then you finish, and there it is —. 
hell yes, I made that.   
That feeling doesn't get old.


The robots come from somewhere deeper and further back.

The 80s gave us robots on every screen, in every cartoon, every movie, and something in me never let go.  

What I keep coming back to is what I can only call fragile immortality.   
Inside that eventually rusting, crumbling shell is all of the accumulated knowledge of the universe.   
Infinite wisdom.   
Infinite time.   
A body that outlasts time.


I'll be honest — I'd transfer myself into a robot without hesitation. 

Live forever. 

See the vastness of space up close and personal. 

Though maybe robotical isn’t the right word, since I'd be inside a matrix framework piloting a mechanical body.   
Then again — isn't that already what humans are?   
A consciousness piloting a weak meat suit that's perpetually out of tune with the world around it.


I always knew I wanted to make things. Always did.   
It isn't a choice or a career path.   
It's an instinct.   
A force of nature,   
beyond good and evil.  

Who am I to disobey this great magnetic system of creation and joy.
